1. What you get

Two things that are easy to confuse. The card is a physical object you own outright once we have handed it to you, the same way you own a pen. The page behind it is a service we run for you: an address, a row in our database, and something that answers when a phone taps the card.

The address is chosen when the card is made and then printed on plastic, so it cannot be changed. Everything else — your name, your phone number, your prices, whether the card is visible at all — you change yourself, as often as you like, and the next tap shows it.

2. Your account

One account belongs to one person: the person the card was issued to. You confirm your email address when you first sign in, and that confirmation is what proves the card is yours — so keep the address working and the password to yourself. Anything done from your account is treated as done by you.

You must be at least 18 years old and, if you are acting for a business, authorised to act for it.

3. What you may put on a card

A card is a business card, published under your own name on our domain. So:

  • Say true things about yourself, your work, and your prices.
  • Do not pretend to be somebody else, or to belong to a company you do not.
  • Do not publish anyone else's personal data — their phone number, their photograph, their address — without their agreement.
  • Nothing illegal under Polish or EU law, nothing that infringes somebody's copyright or trade mark, nothing that attacks or demeans people, and nothing linking to any of that.
  • No malware, no phishing, no links whose purpose is to take money or credentials from whoever tapped your card.

What you write stays yours. You give us only the permission we need to run the service — to store it, and to show it to anyone who opens your card — and that permission ends when you delete it.

4. What we may do about it

If a card breaks section 3 we may hide it. For anything ambiguous we will write to you first and ask; for something clearly illegal or harmful we will hide it immediately and tell you afterwards. You can always ask us to look again, and a human will.

Hiding a card does not take the physical card away from you, and it does not take your money: see section 7.

We try hard to keep your card answering every tap, and we built it so that a visitor gets a finished page in one request rather than a loading screen. But we are a small studio running on Google's infrastructure, and we do not promise a particular uptime. The service is provided as it is.

Specifically, we do not promise that the App is free of faults, that it will work on every phone or every version of iOS, or that a page will be reachable during an outage at Google or at your own network. We do not promise that a card will bring you customers.

If the service is going away, we will tell you with as much warning as we can and leave the pages up for as long as we reasonably can after that, because the cards are already in people's pockets.

6. The chip

The chip inside the card is protected with a password we hold, so neither you nor anyone who finds the card can rewrite what it points at. This is deliberate: it is what makes a card you hand to a stranger safe to hand to a stranger.

Please do not try to break that protection. A chip can be permanently destroyed by a bad write, and a destroyed chip means a new card. If you want the address changed, ask us — usually the answer is a new card, and often we will simply make you one.

Cards wear out, get washed, get sat on. A chip that stops working through normal use inside the first year we replace at no charge; after that, or if it has been drilled, cut, or put through a microwave, we will quote you for a new one.
